# Reviewer Attack — P001

Adversarial audit against the 25 attack vectors in §17 of the programme checklist, run as the harshest
plausible SIGIR / CHIIR / IP&M referee. Each entry states the attack, the answer, the evidence, and
whether the paper was changed or an experiment was added. "Fixed with prose" is not an accepted remedy;
where a defect was real, the resolution column records a new run, a lowered claim, or an outstanding gap.

## What survives the attack

The central result is not produced by any of the paths a referee would suspect: one gated test
evaluation, dev-only selection with a single globally frozen configuration, clustered inference, five
seeds, chance-corrected metrics, and an added pre-registered analysis (H4) that came back **against**
the hypothesis and was reported as a null.

## What a referee should still reject if not fixed

1. **No dense/neural retrieval baseline** (A9). Without it the ceiling of the task is unknown and
   "predicts failure" could be read as "predicts the residue a weak ranker leaves".
2. **One dataset family** (A13). Three configs of Toucan-1.5M are not three sources.
3. **Ranker-relative DV with 3.4–10.6% positive overlap** (A4). Quantified, not resolved.
4. **Unseen-catalogue generalisation is now measured, on two axes** (A7/A11a/A13, EXP-0021/0023): catalogue composition alone costs nothing detectable (801 disjoint rows at 0.8316), while unseen tool vocabulary costs 0.118 AUROC (0.6939 cross-corpus vs 0.8118 in domain). The corpus
   cannot supply a catalogue-disjoint test set, so 0.718 on Qwen3 is a conservative surrogate.

Item 4 was created by this audit and is answered as far as these data allow (grouped CV, corrected
clustering, narrowed claim). Items 1-3 need new data or new runs and remain the to-do list in
`limitations.md`. The intervention gap that used to sit here is closed by EXP-0016.
